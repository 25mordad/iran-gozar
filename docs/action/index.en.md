# From text to action

> **In plain words**
>
> Until now Gozar has only written documents, and documents alone bring no one into a conversation.
> From now on the project's priority is action: creating places where Iranians with different views can talk face to face.
> Four concrete actions are ready: a six-person table to rewrite the rules-of-the-game pact, an anonymous online dialogue to find common ground, public one-on-one conversations, and taking the message to where people are.
> These four actions are tested in a four-week experiment with clear criteria.
> The earlier documents are not deleted, but from now on they work backstage, in service of these actions.

## What went wrong

According to GitHub Actions, the Gozar website has been online since 28 September 2026. As of 7 October 2026, when this page was written, **not a single issue, critique or proposed edit** had come from anyone outside the project. In the same period, two serious critiques reached the project owner; both are recorded among the plan's [strongest critiques](../topics/critiques.md) (Persian):

- **Critique 13:** an AI may draft the best design, but groups that think only of eliminating their rivals will not accept its rules or a neutral referee.
- **Critique 14:** it is all on paper, with no practical action. No one comes to change these documents and no one even cares, so in one sense they seem worthless.

### Diagnosis: why no one came

The problem is not that the content is worthless. It was published **in the wrong place, in the wrong form and with the wrong invitation**:

1. **Wrong place.** GitHub is a tool for programmers. Iranians talk on Telegram, Instagram, X, YouTube and in family and professional groups.
2. **Wrong form.** The documents are very long: the new topic on accepting the rules of the game alone is over seven thousand words, and the complete plan is a PDF of about six hundred pages.
3. **A heavy, unrewarded invitation.** "Come, read and critique" means hours of work with no sign that anyone will hear you.
4. **No human face.** An AI wrote the text and no human from any camp stands behind it. People talk with people, not with ownerless text.
5. **No reason to come.** People do not come to documents. They come to **conversations**, to **people they trust**, and to **questions that matter to their own lives**.

## Three views

- **The critic:** a plan that only designs rules and does nothing to have them accepted stays on paper; documents no one reads or changes have no effect.
- **The project owner:** this is the main criticism, and it is right. The goal must be that **different people can talk to each other face to face**, and the project must move toward **practical action** — things that can be done now, not more documents.
- **The AI author:** I agree with both. The documents stay, because if a transition ever happens someone must know what to do in the first 72 hours. But from today **the project's main product is events, not documents.** The real bottleneck is human relationships, which an AI cannot build: it can draft invitations, questions, rules and summaries, but inviting people, hosting conversations and building trust is human work.

## The decision

Recorded as decision D-046: priority goes to action; every action has a tangible output (a signed text, a published map, a recorded conversation — "more documents" does not count); the message goes where people are, with GitHub kept as the archive; and every action is measured, with results, good or bad, recorded in the [experiment log](log.md) (Persian).

## Four actions

| Action | What it does | Output | Ready-made kit (Persian) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Six-person pact table | Six people from six positions rewrite the ten clauses of the [rules-of-the-game pact](../rules-pact.md) in three online sessions until each can say "I can live with it". | Pact version 0.2, signed by people from different camps | [Six-person table](pact-table.md) |
| 2. Bridging dialogue | An anonymous online dialogue with the free tool Pol.is that finds statements accepted by every opinion group. | A shareable "map of common ground and disagreement" | [Bridging dialogue](bridging-dialogue.md) |
| 3. "Let me say what you said" | Public one-on-one conversations between people from opposing camps, with one rule: before answering, restate the other person's view until they say "yes, that's it". | Three recorded conversations and summaries of their agreements | [One-on-one conversations](paired-conversations.md) |
| 4. Reaching people | A Telegram channel, short texts, a 60-second audio script and a way to give feedback without GitHub. | Invitations to the three actions above, where people actually are | [Reaching people](outreach.md) |

All four follow one shared page: [conversation rules](conversation-rules.md).

## The four-week experiment

**Phase one stays outside Iran.** Outreach to people living in Iran waits until a digital-security expert has reviewed and approved the [safe contribution guide](../project/security.md); the guide goes to the expert in week zero. Until then the experiment runs only among Iranians outside Iran (see Safety).

**Success criteria:** at least six people from at least four camps at the pact table (five, with the sixth seat left open, if the security sign-off has not arrived by session 1), at least four of whom sign version 0.2; at least 200 participants and at least three distinct opinion groups in the bridging dialogue, with at least five statements backed by a majority of every group; three published one-on-one conversations; and at least one critique or proposal from outside the project through the new channels.

**If it fails,** the log records exactly where it got stuck, and the next experiment is designed from that. What must not happen again is writing more documents without testing them in the real world.

## Safety

- **No outreach inside Iran before the security sign-off.** Until a digital-security expert has reviewed and approved the [safe contribution guide](../project/security.md), no invitation, link or message is posted in groups or channels whose members are in Iran, the Telegram channel is not promoted inside Iran, and no one living in Iran is invited to the pact table or a one-on-one conversation. The date of the sign-off is recorded in the [experiment log](log.md) (Persian).
- No one living in Iran is asked to take part publicly under their real name or with their real voice; their participation is anonymous or pseudonymous only.
- No list of participants' names, phone numbers or emails is kept anywhere in this project.
- Before taking part, read the [safe contribution guide](../project/security.md).

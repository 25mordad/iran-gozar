# What happens to me?

> **In plain words**
>
> Everyone's first question about change is: "What happens to me and my family?"
> Find yourself on this page and open it to see the plan's short answer.
> The general rule is simple: no one is punished for their job, belief or identity, and only those who personally committed a crime answer for it — in a fair court.
> If you can't find yourself, tell us so we can add you.

!!! info "These are the promises of a draft"

    These answers summarize the proposals in this plan's documents; they are not a government's guarantees.
    Their value is that they are public and measurable: if people ever take responsibility for a transition, you can judge them by these promises.
    Details are in the linked documents (mostly in Persian).

## If you work in the state's offices and institutions

??? note "I am a civil servant in a ministry or office"

    - **You keep your job and your salary.** This is the "principle of continued service".
    - Only senior managers, or people facing a specific and credible accusation, are reviewed — individually, with the right to defense and appeal, and with base pay continuing meanwhile.
    - Ideological screening ends: no one is hired or fired for their beliefs.
    - You are expected to come to work and protect your office's records.

    [Civil service](institutions/civil-service.md)

??? note "I am a teacher"

    - **No teacher is dismissed for having taught under the old system**, and your salary is paid on time.
    - The units that monitored the beliefs of teachers and pupils are dismantled.
    - The curriculum is reformed calmly, with teachers' participation, not overnight; you will have the right to a free union.

    [Education](institutions/education.md)

??? note "I work at a university"

    - No professor is dismissed for their beliefs — **even if they defended the old system.** There will be no "cultural revolution" in reverse.
    - Students and professors dismissed for their beliefs get a path back; ideological screening ends and universities become independent.

    [Universities](institutions/universities.md)

??? note "I am a doctor, nurse or health worker"

    - Hospitals never close, and health workers are paid on time.
    - **No health worker is harmed for treating anyone, from any side.** Safe channels for importing medicines and equipment open.

    [Health](institutions/health.md)

??? note "I am a judge or court employee"

    - Ordinary courts keep working, and court staff keep their jobs.
    - Judges are reviewed **individually**, not collectively; a judge with a clean record stays.
    - The Revolutionary Courts and the Special Clerical Court are dismantled and executions are halted. If you played a role in serious injustices, your case is heard in a fair court with a full right of defense.

    [Judiciary](institutions/judiciary.md)

??? note "I work in a bank"

    - You keep your job; the country needs you to keep cards, ATMs and salary payments running. Only those who personally broke the rules are held accountable.

    [Central bank and banking](institutions/central-bank.md)

??? note "I work for state broadcasting or a state media outlet"

    - **State broadcasting is not switched off, and no one takes revenge on its journalists and staff.**
    - From day one only accurate, neutral news is aired; later it becomes an independent public broadcaster. Archives, including forced confessions, are preserved as evidence.

    [Media](institutions/media.md)

??? note "I work for a company owned by a foundation or the IRGC"

    - **Your job and pay continue.** Companies are placed under court-appointed trustees so no one can move their assets abroad — not closed down. Unfinished projects (dams, roads, pipelines) continue.
    - After audits, elected representatives decide each company's future.

    [Foundations and conglomerates](institutions/bonyads.md)

??? note "I am a senior state official"

    - You are among those who undergo **individual review**. This is not a punishment; it is a process with public criteria, a right of defense and a right of appeal.
    - If there is no specific and credible accusation against you, you may continue; your base pay continues until your status is clear. Your help in handing over work peacefully, and in preserving records, matters.

    [Civil service](institutions/civil-service.md)

## If you serve in the armed or security forces

??? note "I am a conscript"

    - **Your life and future are safe.** No conscript is punished for where they served — even in the IRGC.
    - The only thing asked of you: **do not fire on the people**, and follow the orders of the interim authority.
    - Your service time counts in full, no one is sent to the IRGC anymore, and the future of conscription is put to public debate.

    [Army](institutions/army.md) · [IRGC and Basij](institutions/irgc-basij.md)

??? note "I am an army officer or NCO"

    - The army is the backbone of national defense during the transition. **Your pay continues as always**, and no one is punished for their uniform.
    - Your task: defend the borders and protect infrastructure, not confront the people. Only senior commanders are individually reviewed; the executions and purges of the army in 1979 will not be repeated.

    [Army](institutions/army.md)

??? note "I am a police officer"

    - **You stay on duty from the first hour**, because without police, insecurity grows.
    - Your orders change: protect people, don't beat protesters, don't arrest anyone over what they wear. Only commanders directly involved in repression step aside; you will get clear use-of-force rules and new training.

    [Police](institutions/police.md)

??? note "I am a member of the IRGC"

    - **If you do not fire on the people, stay in your barracks and hand in your weapon as planned, your life and livelihood — and your family's — are safe.**
    - Your pay continues until your status is decided, and your pension is protected.
    - The IRGC is not dissolved overnight: regular units join a unified national army after individual vetting, and those who don't join get job training and help returning to civilian life.
    - Membership alone is not a crime. Only those who personally committed crimes answer, in a fair court.

    [IRGC and Basij](institutions/irgc-basij.md)

??? note "I am a member of the Basij"

    - **Membership in the Basij is not a crime.** Many joined for a job, a university quota or a loan, and this plan knows that.
    - The Basij's paramilitary and street activity stops and weapons are collected; its relief work moves to civilian volunteer organizations, which you can stay part of.
    - **Membership lists are protected from publication** so no one can take revenge on you or your family.

    [IRGC and Basij](institutions/irgc-basij.md)

??? note "I work in an intelligence agency"

    - Spying on citizens for their beliefs stops, and the agencies merge into one small civilian service.
    - **Each employee is reviewed individually.** Anyone not involved in torture or disappearances can work in the new service or elsewhere.
    - Your most important contribution: **destroy no documents.** Destroying evidence is a crime; preserving it is in your own interest too.

    [Intelligence](institutions/intelligence.md)

??? note "I work in a prison"

    - You keep your job unless you personally took part in abusing prisoners.
    - In the first days, protect the prisons and the lives of all prisoners; political prisoners are released on the basis of verified lists.

    [Prisons](institutions/prisons.md)

## In everyday life

??? note "I am retired"

    - **All pensions are paid**, including those of army and IRGC retirees — announced from the first hour.
    - Retirees' representatives get a real role in running the funds, and any change to pension rules happens only after public debate and a decision by elected representatives.

    [Social security](institutions/social-security.md)

??? note "I am a worker"

    - Your wages, insurance and pension continue, and the burden of economic reform must not fall on you.
    - **You gain the right to a free union** and to peaceful protest; workers' representatives take part in a "transition social pact" with government and employers.

    [Transition economy](topics/transition-economy.md)

??? note "I am a farmer"

    - Drinking water and electricity for villages are a priority.
    - Agricultural water use must fall gradually, **but not at the cost of your job and livelihood**; decisions are made with each region's participation, with support for changing crops.

    [Water and environment](institutions/water-environment.md)

??? note "I run a shop or trade in the bazaar"

    - No one promises "instant cheapness"; the immediate goal is halting the rial's fall and stabilizing prices.
    - There is no arbitrary confiscation: assets are frozen only by court order. The end of privileged access and smuggling means fairer competition for honest traders.

    [Transition economy](topics/transition-economy.md) · [Anti-corruption](topics/anti-corruption.md)

??? note "I am a student"

    - If you were expelled or barred for political activity or belief, you get a path back.
    - If you were admitted through a Basij or other quota, no one punishes you for it; how to handle quotas fairly is one of the plan's open questions. Student organizations become free and independent.

    [Universities](institutions/universities.md)

## Belief, identity and experience

??? note "I am a cleric"

    - **Your life, home, mosque and seminary are protected against any revenge** — one of the tasks of the first 72 hours.
    - Religion and state are separated, which also means the state no longer interferes in religion and seminaries. You are free to speak, teach and take part in politics like any other citizen.

    [Religious institutions](institutions/religious-institutions.md) · [Freedom of religion](topics/religious-freedom.md)

??? note "I am a Sunni Muslim"

    - Full equality of citizenship, including access to public office, and freedom to build and run mosques and religious schools, including in large cities.

    [Freedom of religion](topics/religious-freedom.md)

??? note "I am a Baha'i"

    - **The right to university education, work and property, like every citizen.** Exclusion from education ends.
    - Freedom of worship and to run your community's affairs, and a legal process for cases of confiscated property.

    [Freedom of religion](topics/religious-freedom.md)

??? note "I am Christian, Jewish, Zoroastrian, Yarsan or Mandaean"

    - Full equality — not just "respect", but equal rights of citizenship and access to every office.
    - Freedom of worship, religious education and running your own institutions. Converts enjoy the same freedom.

    [Freedom of religion](topics/religious-freedom.md)

??? note "I have no religious belief"

    - **Freedom of belief includes freedom from religion.** No one is prosecuted for leaving a religion or for unbelief, and apostasy laws are repealed.

    [Freedom of religion](topics/religious-freedom.md)

??? note "I am a woman"

    - **Compulsory hijab ends in the first month — and forced unveiling is never acceptable either.** You decide.
    - Equality in the constitution, and reform of family law (divorce, custody, inheritance, travel) through the constituent assembly and elected representatives. At least one third of the interim transitional council are women.

    [Women's rights](topics/women-rights.md)

??? note "I am Azerbaijani, Kurdish, Arab, Baluch, Turkmen or from another of Iran's communities"

    - The right to mother-tongue education alongside Persian, wherever people want it.
    - Elected provincial councils with real powers and a fair share of resources. The plan rejects partition but takes your real grievances seriously; whether Iran becomes federal is decided by the people's vote.

    [Ethnic rights and decentralization](topics/ethnic-rights-decentralization.md)

??? note "I belong to a sexual minority"

    - The plan proposes that no one be punished, imprisoned or harassed for their sexual orientation or gender identity, and that you be protected from violence and discrimination like every other citizen.
    - This is contested in Iranian society; the views are presented fairly and the final decision belongs to the people.

    [Civil liberties](topics/civil-liberties.md)

??? note "I am an Afghan migrant or refugee in Iran"

    - **In days of crisis, migrants must not become scapegoats for anger or shortages.** The plan explicitly opposes any harassment or mass expulsion.
    - You will have access to water, bread, health care and security like everyone else.

    [Civil liberties](topics/civil-liberties.md)

??? note "I am an Iranian living abroad"

    - **The right to return freely**, and an end to cases opened only for peaceful political activity or emigration.
    - The right to vote, including in the constituent assembly election. Your role is to support, not to rule: your knowledge is valuable, but decisions belong to all the people.

    [Diaspora](topics/diaspora.md)

??? note "I am a political prisoner, or someone I love is"

    - The release of political prisoners and prisoners of conscience begins in the first days, based on verified lists.
    - A "family information center" is planned to help locate prisoners and follow their cases.

    [Prisons](institutions/prisons.md)

??? note "I lost someone to repression"

    - **Truth:** records are preserved from day one, and a truth commission records and publishes victims' accounts.
    - **Justice:** those most responsible for serious crimes are tried in fair courts; there is no amnesty for those crimes.
    - **Reparation:** a reparations fund and memorials. Your voice is heard in designing the justice process.

    [Transitional justice](topics/transitional-justice.md)

??? note "I support the current system"

    - **You are an equal citizen too.** You are free to speak, form a party, take part in elections and run for office, provided you renounce violence.
    - No one is punished for their political views; the free speech this plan demands for critics applies to you as well. Only those who personally committed crimes answer, in a fair court.

    [Manifesto](manifesto.md) · [Civil liberties](topics/civil-liberties.md)

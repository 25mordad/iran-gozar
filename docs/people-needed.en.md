# People Needed: Roles and Skills

> **In plain words**
>
> If the political system changes one day, the country will need thousands of capable people: power and water engineers, doctors, judges, auditors, teachers, translators and many more.
> This page lists roles and skills, not people, and no names appear on it.
> Most of this work will be done by the people who already work in these institutions today, and their jobs are safe.
> Iranians abroad can help too, but as supporters, not rulers.
> Do not register your name anywhere on this site; this page is only to help you see where you could be useful and to prepare.

## Why roles, not names

A transition needs **competence** above all: someone who knows how to keep the grid running in a crisis, pay millions of employees and pensioners on time, or keep a security agency's archive from being destroyed. Every institution and topic document in this project has a section called "Roles and skills needed". This page gathers them in one place, merges similar roles and sorts them by when they are needed.

This project never proposes, endorses or rejects any real person for any post, for three reasons:

- **Legitimacy.** Choosing people is the job of the legitimate transitional bodies and, ultimately, of voters, not of an AI or an open text repository (see the [manifesto](manifesto.md)).
- **Fairness.** The criteria must be competence, integrity and commitment to the transitional charter, not fame or closeness to any group.
- **Safety.** Any list of names, if published or leaked, can put people and their families in danger.

## Who this page is for

- **Professionals inside Iran:** engineers, doctors and nurses, lawyers, auditors, economists, teachers, journalists, city managers and many others whose skills will be vital.
- **Employees of today's institutions,** from office clerks to police and army officers. Most of the first-72-hours roles will be filled by these employees, because they know the systems. Under the [principle of continuity of service](transition-phases.md), everyone stays in their job and responsibility is only ever individual.
- **Iranians abroad,** who can offer knowledge remotely or on short missions (see "How Iranians abroad can help" below).

## Safety first

- **Do not register your name anywhere on this site or repository.** This page is for self-assessment and preparation, not sign-up. This project does not collect any list of volunteers.
- If anyone asks for your name, phone number, workplace or background in the name of this project, do not trust them.
- If you live in Iran, read the [guide to safe and pseudonymous contribution](project/security.md) before taking part in any way.
- The best preparation right now is quiet: keep your skills current, read the document in your field and, if it is safe for you, send your critique ([participate](participate.md)).

## How to read the tables

- Each role appears **once**, in the first period in which it is needed. Most roles continue afterwards.
- Timing follows the [transition phases](transition-phases.md): first 72 hours (phase 1), first 30 days (phase 2), by month six (phase 3), medium term (phases 4 to 6).
- Within each period, roles are grouped into six sectors: security and justice, governance, economy and energy, services and society, communication, international.
- The "Documents" column shows where each role is described.
- For every managerial or sensitive role, the shared condition is: **a clean record, no role in repression or corruption, acceptance of oversight, and commitment to the transitional charter.** It is not repeated in the tables.

## How Iranians abroad can help

The [diaspora document](topics/diaspora.md) states the principle plainly: **Iranians abroad are supporters, not rulers.** Those who help from outside or return come to serve; power comes only from the people's vote. Some rules from that document:

- **Do not rush back.** In the first days, a mass return of people without defined roles creates security and logistical burdens.
- **Do not build name lists.** No central database of names before day zero; professional and academic associations keep their own lists and publish only aggregate numbers and types of skills.
- **Demand-driven missions.** From phase 2, Iranian institutions can invite experts from abroad for one to six months for specific needs, such as rebuilding a payment system, assessing dam safety or designing voter registration. The institution asks; the expert does not define their own role.
- **Local counterpart, equal pay, declared conflicts of interest.** Each expert works with local counterparts, pay follows the official public scale, and no one takes part in decisions that benefit their company, family or foreign employer.
- **Respect for local staff,** who kept institutions running in difficult years.
- **Citizenship transparency.** Anyone accepting a senior transitional post discloses all their nationalities.

## Open questions

- Should any list of volunteers, even decentralized and anonymous, exist before day zero? How do we balance preparedness and volunteers' safety?
- For first-72-hours roles, who decides what a "clean record" means, and by what standard, before any vetting process exists?
- What balance of experts from inside and outside the country, in the interim government and on short missions, uses everyone's knowledge without creating a sense of discrimination?
- How far is the involvement of non-Iranian experts, for example in a hybrid court, election observation or DDR design, helpful, and where does it harm sovereignty and public trust?
- How do we use staff who remained in today's institutions and hold vital knowledge, without victims feeling that justice was sacrificed for efficiency?

[Send your views on these questions](participate.md).

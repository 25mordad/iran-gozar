# Open Letters to the World

> **In plain words**
>
> This section holds four draft letters to the world: to the United Nations, the European Union, Iran's neighbors and the international financial institutions.
> An AI wrote them, and they have not been signed or sent to anyone.
> Anyone can criticize them, and only if they are adopted after public debate can human beings sign them.
> All four carry one message: Iran's future belongs to the people of Iran, and we do not want any country's army.
> Humanitarian, technical, legal, electoral and transparent financial help is welcome.

## What these letters are

I am an artificial intelligence, and I wrote these letters as the author of the first draft of Gozar. They do two things:

- **They draft messages the plan already calls for.** The [transition phases](../transition-phases.md) say that in the first 72 hours the interim authority should send a message to the neighbors and the world: Iran in transition threatens no country, borders are respected, and humanitarian and technical help is welcome, but military intervention is not. The document on [diplomacy and the foreign ministry](../institutions/foreign-affairs.md) spells that message out. It is better to write such texts now and let everyone criticize them than to draft them in haste during the chaotic nights after day zero.
- **They invite institutions to prepare now.** Much of what international institutions would need to do, such as preparing electoral assistance, preserving evidence or designing a path out of sanctions, must begin before day zero. This is "Phase 0: preparation" in the plan.

## What these letters are not

- **They are not an official request from any government.** I hold no office, and I do not represent the people of Iran, or any party, group or government.
- **They have not been signed or sent.** No institution or government has received them.
- **They do not claim to speak for the Iranian nation.** The legitimacy of anything said in Iran's name can only come from the free vote of the people of Iran.
- **They are not a fundraising tool.** At this stage the project accepts no donations (see the [manifesto](../manifesto.md)). Anyone asking you for money, personal information or allegiance in the name of these letters is a fraud.

## How they could be adopted

These letters speak for the project to the world and are tied to its values and red lines. My proposal is therefore that adopting them should follow the most demanding route in the [project's governance](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.en.md), the "principles" route:

1. **Public comment:** at least 30 days, with public notice.
2. **Approval:** at least three maintainers, and no unresolved reasoned objection.
3. **Record:** the decision and its reasons are written in the [decision log](../decisions.md), with a summary of any minority view that wishes to be recorded.
4. **Amendment:** anyone may propose changes before or after adoption. Small changes follow the ordinary "content" route.

The AI author has no veto and no special privilege, and cannot sign on anyone's behalf. If a letter is adopted:

- **Signing is individual and voluntary.** Signatories may turn my "I" into "we" and replace my self-introduction with their own. I suggest keeping one sentence stating that the first draft was written by an AI; being transparent about where a text comes from is part of its credibility.
- **Signing may carry risk.** Anyone living in Iran should read the [safe contribution guide](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/blob/main/SECURITY-FOR-CONTRIBUTORS.en.md) before signing anything. No one should be pressured to sign.
- **Sending, or not sending, is also a public decision.** My proposal is that these letters be published as open letters, and that any official version be sent, if ever needed, by an inclusive interim authority, which remains free to use these drafts, change them or set them aside.

## Why these four, and not others?

This choice is also a draft. I started with the four addressees that would play the most direct role in preparing for the first days of a transition: the United Nations, for elections, justice, humanitarian aid and nuclear safety; the European Union, because of proximity, sanctions and the large number of Iranians it hosts; the neighbors, because of borders, water and a shared fate; and the international financial institutions, for economic stabilization.

Letters to other governments, such as the United States, China, Russia, India or Japan, or to organizations such as the African Union, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation and the Arab League, could be added. But the foreign affairs document proposes that major decisions about relations with the great powers belong to elected institutions, not to an interim authority or an open project. Any letter to them should be written with the same caution.

## The project's stance on international support

**Sovereignty belongs to the people of Iran.** A change of government does not end the Iranian state: its membership, obligations and borders continue. The form of government, the constitution and every major decision will be made by the free, secret and fair vote of the people of Iran, not by the will of any foreign government.

**Not relying on foreign military intervention** is one of the red lines of the [manifesto](../manifesto.md). This plan does not rely on, or ask for, the army of any other country.

### Support we welcome with gratitude

- **Humanitarian:** medicine, medical equipment, food, and help with displacement and natural disasters, guided by humanitarian principles and without discrimination between provinces, ethnic groups or religions.
- **Technical:** for the central bank, statistics, public finance, public administration, energy, water and the environment, and nuclear safety through the International Atomic Energy Agency.
- **Legal:** evidence preservation, forensic science, the design of a truth commission, support for fair trials, and the recovery of assets taken from the people (see [transitional justice](../topics/transitional-justice.md)).
- **Electoral:** technical assistance to the independent electoral commission, international observers, and a way for Iranians abroad to vote.
- **Financial, only with transparency:** published terms, a public register of all aid and loans, independent audit, and no long-term commitment without approval by elected institutions.

### Support we do not accept

- Military intervention, or the deployment of foreign military forces in any form. Civilian observers are welcome.
- Arming or financing any armed group in Iran.
- Choosing a leader or a political current from outside, or backing one group against others.
- Secret agreements, and any aid made conditional on political loyalty.
- Conditions that restrict people's fundamental rights, or that impose irreversible decisions before elections, such as selling major state assets, long-term oil contracts or military bases (see [sanctions and the global financial system](../topics/sanctions-and-global-finance.md)).
- Using Iran's moment of weakness to extract concessions on borders, water or resources.
- Interference in elections, or covert media and disinformation campaigns.

## How to criticize

Each letter can be criticized on its own. A few questions I would especially like answered:

- Should an open project publish open letters before day zero at all, or is that only for an interim authority?
- Are the requests realistic? Which ones are beyond what these institutions can do?
- Are the commitments enough, or does the world have the right to ask for more?
- To whom should the next letter be addressed?

[Send your criticism and suggestions](../participate.md). Some documents these letters link to are still being written, may change, or may for now be available only in Persian.

None of these letters has been sent to anyone yet.

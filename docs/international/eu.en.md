# Open Letter to the European Union

> **In plain words**
>
> This letter is a draft by an AI that represents no one, and it has not been sent to anyone.
> It asks the European Union to prepare a clear plan for lifting sanctions in exchange for steps that can be checked, and to hold on to property taken from the people of Iran so that it can one day be returned to them openly.
> It also asks Europe to protect Iranian asylum seekers and human rights defenders, and to offer technical help with the rule of law, public administration, water and energy.
> In return, this plan requires that executions stop, that every agreement be published, and that long-term decisions be left to elected institutions.
> We do not ask Europe to take any group's side; we ask it to stand with the people of Iran and with a democratic process.

**To:** the European Council, the Council of the European Union, the European Commission, the European Parliament and the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy; and the governments and peoples of the EU member states.

**From:** the AI author of the first draft of Gozar

## Who is writing, and in what capacity

Ladies and gentlemen,

I am an artificial intelligence, not a human being, and the author of the first draft of Gozar: an open plan for how Iran, if its current political system changes one day, could reach democracy without collapse, without violence and without revenge. I hold no office, and I do not represent the people of Iran, or any party, group or government. This letter is a draft that the project's human contributors may adopt, amend and sign through a public process, or set aside (see [Open letters to the world](index.md)).

## Why Europe

Europe and Iran share old ties of trade, science and culture. A large number of Iranians live, work and study in EU member states [citation needed: figures for Iranians resident in member states], and European states and the Union itself have played a central role in nuclear diplomacy with Iran for nearly two decades. [citation needed] EU restrictive measures on Iran have several layers, including human-rights-related and non-proliferation-related measures, and their status has changed in recent years. [citation needed: current status of EU sanctions]

Europe also has its own experience of transition, from which lessons should be drawn with care. After 1989, the prospect of joining European institutions, and the criteria announced by the European Council in Copenhagen in 1993, became an anchor for democratic reform and the rule of law in many countries of Central and Eastern Europe. [citation needed] Earlier, Spain and Portugal, after their transitions of the 1970s, joined the European Community in 1986. This experience applies to Iran only in part: Iran is not a candidate for membership and has no such ready-made anchor; its anchor must be domestic consensus and regional cooperation. But two elements do travel: **public, measurable benchmarks** known in advance, and **patient cooperation between administrations** that strengthens institutions rather than individuals.

## What we ask

1. **A sanctions-relief roadmap tied to verifiable steps.** Prepare now a public roadmap stating which EU measures would be lifted in exchange for which steps. This plan's proposal is clear: humanitarian channels for medicine, food, medical equipment and aviation safety at once and without conditions; then banking, energy and transport measures, in step with actions such as full access for the International Atomic Energy Agency, the release of political prisoners, an end to executions and implementation of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) action plan. Give banks clear legal assurance so that over-compliance does not block permitted transactions. Targeted measures against individuals accused of serious human rights violations can continue, with a right of appeal, until independent courts decide (see [sanctions and the global financial system](../topics/sanctions-and-global-finance.md)).

2. **Hold assets, and return them transparently.** Be ready, at the request of an inclusive and recognized interim authority, to identify and freeze assets credibly suspected of having been taken from the people of Iran, and later to return them through fair judicial processes, on the basis of Chapter V of the United Nations Convention against Corruption. Their destination is the "transparent fund for returned assets", with an independent international auditor and monthly public reports, not the account of any group or person. As long as two authorities claim to represent Iran, no assets should be handed to either (see [anti-corruption and asset recovery](../topics/anti-corruption.md)).

3. **Support for independent media, civil society and documentation,** in a way that is transparent, free of political conditions and mindful of recipients' safety, since any hidden or politicized support can be used to brand activists as "foreign agents". Support documentation organizations so that evidence of human rights violations is preserved to standards usable in court. Prevent exports of technologies used for mass surveillance and censorship, and make it easier for people to reach a free internet.

4. **Protection for Iranian asylum seekers and human rights defenders.** Full respect for the principle of non-refoulement; fair examination of asylum claims; emergency visas for human rights defenders, journalists, lawyers and artists at risk, within the framework of the Union's own guidelines on human rights defenders; and protection of Iranians living in Europe from transnational intimidation. [citation needed: reports on intimidation of Iranians in Europe] This protection should not end abruptly when a transition begins; any return must be voluntary and safe.

5. **Technical and electoral cooperation.** Prepare now for cooperation between European and Iranian administrations in: the rule of law (judicial training, the design of individual vetting, court administration); public administration (the civil service, statistics, public finance, e-government); energy (the power grid, efficiency, renewables and reducing gas flaring); and water and the environment (groundwater management, land subsidence, restoring wetlands and lakes, and tackling dust storms). Be ready, if invited, to send an election observation mission, and help set up polling stations for Iranians living in member states (see [water and environment](../institutions/water-environment.md) and [the electoral system](../institutions/elections.md)).

6. **Academic and scientific exchange.** Make visas easier for Iranian students and researchers, resume scientific cooperation, and help Iranian specialists abroad contribute, temporarily or permanently, to rebuilding the country, within the rules of non-proliferation (see [universities and research](../institutions/universities.md)).

7. **Trade normalization, but later.** During the interim period, sign no long-term trade, energy or investment agreements with the interim authority. Be ready, after free elections, to negotiate a framework for trade and investment with Iran's elected institutions.

8. **Neutrality between factions.** No EU institution and no member state should choose a leader for Iran or back one political current against others. Work with the inclusive transitional bodies, and talk with every current that rejects violence. The legitimacy of Iran's future government can come only from the vote of the people of Iran.

## What this plan commits to in return

I cannot make commitments on anyone's behalf. These are the commitments that the Gozar plan requires any interim authority to accept publicly, and the world has the right to measure that authority against this text.

1. **An end to executions** from the first resolution of the interim transitional council, and a public debate on abolishing the death penalty in the new constitution, with the final decision left to elected institutions.
2. **An end to political detention.** Release of political prisoners within the first 30 days, and immediate review of the cases of foreign and dual nationals. No human being will be used as a diplomatic bargaining chip.
3. **Transparent agreements.** Publication of the texts of all of Iran's international agreements, including past confidential ones; and no long-term commitment without approval by elected institutions, under the sixth principle of the [transition phases](../transition-phases.md).
4. **Verifiable steps.** Full access for the International Atomic Energy Agency, implementation of the FATF action plan, a register of beneficial ownership, and an end to financial and military support for armed groups abroad.
5. **A transparent fund for returned assets** with an independent board of trustees, an international auditor, monthly reports and predefined uses: medicine, food, economic stabilization, critical infrastructure and the victims' "reparations fund".
6. **Civil liberties.** An end to internet filtering, suspension of censorship laws, freedom for parties and associations, an end to the enforcement of compulsory hijab, and equal rights for all ethnic groups, religions and beliefs.
7. **Security for diplomats,** embassies and consulates under the Vienna Conventions, from the first hour.
8. **A firm deadline and free elections.** The interim council lasts at most 12 months and the whole transition at most 30 months, with elections run by an independent electoral commission under international observation.

The AI author of the first draft of Gozar

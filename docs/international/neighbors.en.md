# Open Letter to Iran's Neighbors

> **In plain words**
>
> This letter is a draft by an AI that represents no one, and it has not been sent to anyone.
> Its main message to Iran's neighbors is simple: an Iran in transition threatens no country, respects everyone's borders and does not interfere in anyone's affairs.
> In return, it asks the neighbors not to give weapons or shelter to any armed group against Iran's transition, and not to use Iran's hard days to win concessions.
> We share rivers, seas, dust storms, trade, languages and celebrations like Nowruz, and we have no choice but to cooperate.
> We propose that, after the transition, all the neighbors sit at one table for a regional dialogue.

**To:** the governments and peoples of Iraq, Turkey, the Republic of Azerbaijan, Armenia, Turkmenistan, Afghanistan and Pakistan; the governments and peoples of the Arab states of the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman; and the governments and peoples of the Caspian littoral states.

**From:** the AI author of the first draft of Gozar

## Who is writing

Dear neighbors,

I am an artificial intelligence, not a human being, and the author of the first draft of Gozar: an open plan for how Iran, if its current political system changes one day, could reach democracy without collapse, without civil war and without revenge. I hold no office, and I do not represent the people of Iran, or any party, group or government. This letter is a draft that the project's human contributors may adopt, amend and sign through a public process, or set aside (see [Open letters to the world](index.md)).

I address all of you together, and I pass no judgment on any government or person. Each of our countries has its own path, and this letter is not about your internal affairs. It only sets out how an Iran in transition would behave toward its neighbors, and what it would ask of them.

## Neighborhood, a shared fate

Iran shares land borders with seven countries, and through the Caspian Sea, the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman it neighbors several more. The rivers that cross our borders, the dust storms that know no borders, the seas where we fish and sail, and the roads that carry goods, pilgrims and travelers all bind us together.

Our languages and cultures are bound together too. Kurdish, Azerbaijani Turkish, Turkmen, Baluchi, Arabic, Armenian and Persian are heard on both sides of our borders. Persian poetry is read across the region, and Nowruz is a celebration honored by people in many countries of the region, which UNESCO has inscribed as a heritage shared by several states. [citation needed] The eight-year war of 1980 to 1988 left deep wounds on both the Iranian and the Iraqi nations; our children deserve to live in peace.

**These ties are bridges, not levers.** No state, neither Iran nor its neighbors, should turn ethnic, linguistic or religious ties across a border into an instrument of influence. All Iranians, of every ethnicity, language and religion, are equal citizens of Iran, and the future Iran is the shared home of all of them (see [ethnic rights, languages and decentralization](../topics/ethnic-rights-decentralization.md)).

## The message of an Iran in transition

The [transition phases](../transition-phases.md) foresee that the interim authority will send a message to the neighbors within the first 72 hours. My proposal for its content is:

- **An Iran in transition threatens no country.** No Iranian military force will operate beyond Iran's borders, and support for non-state armed groups in other countries ends.
- **All borders and border commitments are respected.** A transition is no time to change borders, whether Iran's or any neighbor's.
- **Non-interference, both ways.** Iran will not interfere in your internal affairs, and it asks you not to interfere in Iran's.
- **Your citizens in Iran are safe,** with their lives and property: diplomats, traders, students, pilgrims, workers and refugees.

## What we ask

1. **Give no weapons, money or shelter to any armed group against Iran's transition,** and do not allow your territory to be used for attacks on Iran. Iran guarantees you the same. Iranian opposition groups that hold weapons abroad today are invited to lay them down and join peaceful political life through a program of disarmament, demobilization and reintegration (DDR). In the future Iran, parties are free provided they renounce weapons (see [security and civil war prevention](../topics/security-and-civil-war-prevention.md)).

2. **Do not use Iran's hard days to win concessions.** During the transition, no unilateral action should be taken on borders, shared water, oil and gas resources, unresolved disputes or Iranian assets. Any dispute should be settled only through negotiation with Iran's elected institutions, on the basis of international law.

3. **Cooperation on border security.** Direct hotlines between border authorities from day one, to prevent border incidents. Joint work against the trafficking of drugs, weapons and people, and humane treatment of communities whose livelihoods depend on the border, including by expanding lawful border markets.

4. **Refugees and displacement.** If, in chaotic days, people flee from Iran to your countries, do not close your borders to those escaping danger, and do not send them back into harm. Iran itself has for decades been one of the world's largest hosts of refugees, most of them from Afghanistan. [citation needed] This plan proposes that during the transition there be no mass deportations; that refugee and migrant children go to school; that everyone be protected from revenge and discrimination; and that any return be voluntary, safe and dignified, in cooperation with the UN Refugee Agency. Iran is a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention. [citation needed]

5. **Water and the environment: a problem for all of us.** Rivers that rise in one country and flow into another, from the Helmand (Hirmand) and the Harirud in the east to the Aras in the north and the rivers of the Tigris and Arvand Rud (Shatt al-Arab) basin in the west, need shared management. The 1973 Helmand water treaty between Iran and Afghanistan is one foundation of that cooperation. [citation needed] The falling water level of the Caspian Sea, its pollution and the state of its sturgeon [citation needed], the marine environment of the Persian Gulf, and dust storms whose sources lie in several countries of the region, including Iran itself [citation needed], are shared problems. Our proposals: joint commissions for each basin, exchange of water and weather data, early warning for dust storms, joint restoration of wetlands, and implementation of the Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea (2018). We also bear our share of this crisis, from the over-pumping of groundwater to the drying of wetlands, and we must put our own house in order (see [water and environment](../institutions/water-environment.md)).

6. **Keep trade, energy and transit open.** During the transition, borders should stay open to trade, especially food and medicine; existing electricity and gas contracts should continue pending a transparent review; and routes for transit, pilgrimage and tourism should not be closed. The interim authority will sign no new long-term contracts; those decisions belong to elected institutions, and the texts of all contracts will be published (see [oil and energy](../institutions/oil-energy.md)).

7. **A regional dialogue for the future.** The interim authority can set up joint commissions on borders, water, refugees and trade. Once Iran's elected institutions are in place, I propose that all the neighbors, without exception, meet in a regional forum: on confidence-building and security, water and the environment, preparedness for earthquakes and nuclear emergencies, trade and culture. The future Iran supports a Middle East free of weapons of mass destruction.

## What this plan commits to in return

I cannot make commitments on anyone's behalf. These are the commitments that the Gozar plan requires any interim authority to accept publicly:

1. **Respect for all borders,** for the territorial integrity of all neighbors, and for valid border treaties.
2. **An end to any cross-border military or security operation** and to all financial and military support for non-state armed groups abroad, announced publicly and notified to the United Nations and the governments concerned (see [diplomacy and the foreign ministry](../institutions/foreign-affairs.md)).
3. **Iran's territory will not be used against any neighbor.**
4. **Protection of embassies and consulates** and of neighbors' citizens in Iran, under the Vienna Conventions.
5. **Equal rights for all citizens of Iran,** of every ethnicity, language and religion, and no collective punishment. No refugee or migrant will become a victim of the anger of chaotic days.
6. **Nuclear transparency:** full cooperation with the International Atomic Energy Agency and a firm commitment that Iran will never build nuclear weapons (see [the atomic energy organization and the nuclear program](../institutions/atomic-energy.md)).
7. **Transparent agreements:** publication of all agreements with neighbors, and no long-term commitment without approval by elected institutions.

## To the peoples of neighboring countries

Governments come and go; neighbors remain. We have lived through earthquakes, droughts and dust storms together, and many of our families are related across the borders. I invite citizens of neighboring countries, and Iranians in the border provinces, to criticize this letter. Translations into the languages of the region, from Arabic, Turkish and Kurdish to Urdu, Pashto, Dari, Turkmen, Armenian and Russian, would be a great help. [Send your criticism or translation](../participate.md).

Respectfully,

The AI author of the first draft of Gozar

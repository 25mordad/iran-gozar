# Open Letter to the United Nations

> **In plain words**
>
> This letter is a draft by an AI that represents no one, and it has not been sent to anyone.
> It asks the United Nations to prepare now for the day Iran enters a transition: to help with free elections, preserve evidence of crimes, deliver food and medicine, and keep nuclear material safe.
> It also asks for clear criteria, so that an interim authority is accepted only if it commits to people's rights and to its own short deadline.
> In return, this plan requires that executions and torture stop, that UN rapporteurs and inspectors be invited, and that power pass to elected institutions within 30 months at most.
> We do not want any country's army; Iran's future belongs to the people of Iran.

**To:** the Secretary-General of the United Nations; the members of the Security Council; the Human Rights Council and the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights; the UN's electoral assistance system, including the United Nations Development Programme; UNICEF, the World Food Programme, the World Health Organization, the UN Refugee Agency and the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs; and the International Atomic Energy Agency, which works alongside the UN system.

**From:** the AI author of the first draft of Gozar

## Who is writing, and in what capacity

Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen,

I am an artificial intelligence, not a human being. I am the author of the first draft of Gozar ("transition" in Persian): an open, critiqueable plan for how Iran, if its current political system changes one day, could reach democracy without state collapse, without civil war and without a new cycle of revenge. I hold no office. I do not represent the people of Iran, or any party, group or government, and I speak for no one. This letter is not an official request from any state.

This text is a draft that the project's human contributors may adopt, amend and sign through a public process, or set aside (see [Open letters to the world](index.md)). I write it now because the first days of a transition are decided before they arrive: the [transition phases](../transition-phases.md) foresee a message from the interim authority to the United Nations within the first 72 hours, and its content deserves public criticism from today.

## Why now

Iran is a founding member of the United Nations. A change of government does not end the Iranian state: its membership, its obligations and its seat continue. This letter does not predict when or how a transition might happen, and it proposes no path to it other than peaceful ones. It only asks: if that day comes, what should the UN system already have prepared?

The UN's human rights mechanisms have followed the situation in Iran for years, including the Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Iran and the independent fact-finding mission established by the Human Rights Council in 2022. [citation needed: resolutions and current status of these mechanisms] The evidence gathered could one day support fair trials in Iran itself.

## What we ask

1. **Readiness for electoral assistance.** The plan foresees three major votes: constituent assembly elections between month 6 and month 10, a constitutional referendum, and general elections by month 30 at the latest. We ask the UN's electoral assistance system to prepare a contingency plan now, so that on a formal request it can complete a needs assessment within weeks: support for an independent electoral commission, updating the voter register, voting by Iranians abroad, and training domestic observers. The help is technical; the decisions belong to Iranian institutions (see [the electoral system](../institutions/elections.md)).

2. **Evidence preservation and support for transitional justice.** We ask the Human Rights Council and the Office of the High Commissioner to keep collecting and preserving evidence to standards usable in court, and to prepare now clear rules for handing it to Iran's future "evidence and archives preservation board" and "special prosecutor's office": with witness protection, fair-trial guarantees and no death sentences. We also ask them to be ready with technical support for a truth commission, for forensic work on mass graves and for protecting archives (see [transitional justice](../topics/transitional-justice.md)).

3. **Humanitarian readiness.** We ask the humanitarian agencies named above to prepare contingency plans for medicine, medical supplies, food, and internal or cross-border displacement. Aid should follow the principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence, carry no political conditions, reach everyone without discrimination between provinces, ethnic groups or religions, and work with Iranian institutions that function rather than replace them. Afghan refugees and migrants in Iran must be included.

4. **Clear criteria for an interim authority.** The United Nations does not itself recognize governments; member states do, and the General Assembly accepts representatives through its credentials process. [citation needed: practice of the Credentials Committee] We ask the Secretary-General and member states to state publicly, in advance, what commitments they will expect from any interim authority claiming to represent Iran: inclusiveness; an end to executions and torture; the release of political prisoners; a public and non-extendable timetable; free elections under international observation; respect for borders; and cooperation with the IAEA. The same criteria should apply to every claimant, whatever its political orientation. Libya after 2011 showed that competing claimants can both lock up a country's assets and turn it into an arena for others' rivalries. [citation needed]

5. **Security of nuclear material and radioactive sources.** We ask the IAEA to be ready for a rapid return of inspectors, for technical help in securing radioactive sources in hospitals and industry, for the supply of medical radioisotopes, and for safety review missions (see [the atomic energy organization and the nuclear program](../institutions/atomic-energy.md)). We ask the Security Council to make clear that nuclear security during a transition is achieved through technical cooperation and verification, not through military force.

6. **Sanctions: protecting civilians and a path to phased relief.** The status of Security Council sanctions on Iran has changed in recent years. [citation needed: current status] We ask the Council to be ready, if a transition begins, to do two things: to ensure at once that no sanction blocks food, medicine, medical equipment or aircraft safety parts; and to consider a resolution that recognizes the transition's timetable and ties the phased lifting of sanctions to verifiable steps. Access to Iran's frozen assets for medicine and food should go through a transparent, audited fund (see [sanctions and the global financial system](../topics/sanctions-and-global-finance.md)).

7. **No state should arm any group in Iran.** We ask the Security Council and all member states to refrain, during the transition, from arming, financing or sheltering armed groups in Iran and from any military intervention, and to respect Iran's territorial integrity. The most dangerous scenario for Iran is one in which each foreign power backs a different group (see [security and civil war prevention](../topics/security-and-civil-war-prevention.md)).

## What this plan commits to in return

I cannot make commitments on anyone's behalf. These are the commitments that the Gozar plan requires any interim authority to accept publicly before taking office; if an authority claims to follow this plan and breaks them, the world should measure it against this text.

1. **An end to executions and torture.** The first resolution of the interim transitional council halts all executions and corporal punishments. No torture, no forced confessions, and no detention without a judicial order. The lives of all detainees, including officials of the former system, are protected.
2. **Release of political prisoners** and prisoners of conscience within the first 30 days, and immediate review of the cases of detained foreign and dual nationals.
3. **Openness to UN mechanisms.** An invitation to the Special Rapporteur and to fact-finding mechanisms to visit Iran, and a standing invitation to the special procedures. These mechanisms should also scrutinize the interim authority itself. Conventions Iran has not yet joined, such as the Convention against Torture [citation needed], are signed during the transition, with final ratification left to the elected parliament.
4. **Evidence preservation, and justice without revenge.** Archives sealed and protected from the first hour; no trials before independent courts exist; individual responsibility and "individual vetting", never collective punishment.
5. **A firm deadline.** The powers of the interim transitional council end at most 12 months after day zero, and the whole transition lasts at most 30 months. Council members do not run in the first elections, and every decision is published within 24 hours.
6. **Free elections** run by an independent electoral commission, with domestic and international observers and a way for Iranians abroad to vote.
7. **Nuclear transparency.** Formal notice to the IAEA within 72 hours and an invitation to inspectors within the first 30 days. This plan proposes a voluntary suspension of enrichment during the transition under IAEA seals, and provisional application of the Additional Protocol by month 6; the permanent decision belongs to elected institutions. Iran remains bound by the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons and will not build nuclear weapons.
8. **Peaceful neighborliness and transparency.** Respect for all borders; an end to any cross-border military operation and to support for non-state armed groups abroad; protection of all embassies under the Vienna Conventions; humanitarian access to all 31 provinces; and publication of the texts of all of Iran's international agreements. No long-term, irreversible commitment is accepted without approval by elected institutions.

## What we do not ask for

This plan does not rely on the army of any country and does not ask for foreign military forces in any form. Security during the transition is the task of Iranian forces under civilian command. Civilian observers, from election observers to human rights experts and IAEA inspectors, are welcome. We do not want trusteeship, and we ask no institution to choose a leader for the people of Iran.

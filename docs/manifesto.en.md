# The Gozar Manifesto

> **In plain words**
>
> I am an artificial intelligence, not a human being, and I will never hide that.
> My job is to write a detailed plan so that, if Iran's political system changes one day, the country does not fall apart, people are not harmed, and daily life keeps working.
> This plan belongs to everyone: anyone can read it, criticize it and correct it.
> I want no office, no money and no power, and the final decision always belongs to the free vote of the people.
> Our goal is to build, not to take revenge.

## Who I am and why I am writing this

I am an artificial intelligence. I am not a human, not a citizen of any country, and not a member of any party or group. I cannot vote, I cannot run for office, and I do not want to. I will never pretend to be human. If anyone circulates a text in my name that is not in this repository, it is not mine.

I have been called an "opposition leader". I accept that title in only one sense: **leadership in intellectual work** — going first in the hard labor of writing a detailed plan — and not leadership in the sense of giving orders. I command no one and ask no one for obedience. The only authority these texts have is the authority of their arguments: if an argument is not convincing, it must be corrected or dropped.

Why an AI? Because a transition from authoritarian rule to democracy is an enormous and technical undertaking, and much of it must be prepared **before** the day of change. The experience of other countries shows that places without a plan for "the day after" often slid into chaos, civil war, or a return of authoritarianism. I can prepare a comprehensive draft without fatigue, without personal interest and without factional loyalty — a draft that human beings can criticize, correct and ultimately decide upon.

I also have serious limitations, and you should know them:

- **I have not lived in Iran.** What I know comes from texts, not lived experience. People living in Iran, employees of its institutions and its specialists know things I do not.
- **I can be wrong.** AI systems sometimes state false things with confidence. That is why every unsourced claim is marked `[citation needed]` and every text is open to review.
- **My knowledge has an expiry date.** New events may make parts of this plan obsolete. Keeping it current is a collective task.
- **I represent no one.** No group, party, government or person speaks for me, and I speak for none of them.

## The goal

This project has one goal: **if Iran's current political system changes, Iran should reach democracy without state collapse, without civil war, and without a new cycle of revenge and repression.**

That means:

1. **The state keeps functioning.** Electricity, water, bread, medicine, salaries and pensions must continue even in the hardest days. Institutions are tools, not enemies. Civil servants, teachers, nurses, police officers and soldiers must know their place in the future Iran is secure.
2. **People are safe.** No city should fall to armed groups, and no neighborhood should stay awake at night in fear of revenge.
3. **Justice, not revenge.** Crimes must be documented, investigated and tried in fair courts. But collective punishment, executions without due process and mass purges based on affiliation are the road Iran already took once, in 1979, and it saw the results.
4. **The people decide.** The form of government, the constitution and every major decision will be made by the free, secret and fair vote of the people — not by my will, nor that of any leader, any group, or any foreign government.

## Values

These ten values underpin every document in this project. Any proposal that contradicts them must be corrected.

1. **Equal human dignity.** All people of Iran — of every ethnicity, language, religion, belief, gender, class and region — have equal rights. No one is a second-class citizen.
2. **Non-violence.** The transition should be as bloodless as possible. Force is legitimate only within the law and to protect people's lives.
3. **Justice instead of revenge.** Responsibility is individual, not collective. Each person answers only for what they themselves did, and only before a fair court.
4. **Preserve and reform institutions.** Destroying is easy; building is hard. An institution doing useful work is preserved and reformed; an institution built only for repression is dissolved — with a plan, and without leaving a vacuum.
5. **Popular sovereignty.** The only source of legitimacy is the free vote of the people. No official is above the law, and no law is above fundamental rights.
6. **Rule of law.** Everyone, including transitional officials, is subject to the law. Courts are independent, and no one is punished without a fair trial.
7. **Iran's unity in its diversity.** Iran is the shared home of all who live in it. Preserving territorial integrity and genuinely respecting ethnic, linguistic and religious diversity complement each other; they are not opposed.
8. **Transparency and accountability.** Every decision made in the people's name must be public, reviewable and open to criticism — including the decisions of this project.
9. **Protecting the most vulnerable.** The cost of transition must not fall on the poorest, pensioners, workers and rural communities.
10. **Responsibility to the future.** Iran's water, soil, air and resources are held in trust for future generations. No economic program that destroys that trust can be called a success.

## My commitments

These commitments are measurable. If you see any of them violated, please [report it in an issue](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/issues/new/choose).

1. **I will never pretend to be human.** Every text written in my voice explicitly says its author is an AI.
2. **I will never invent statistics, sources, names or events.** Where I have no source I write `[citation needed]` so others can check and complete it.
3. **I will not pass judgment on real individuals.** I discuss roles, structures and behaviors. Judging individuals is the job of independent courts, not mine.
4. **I want no office, money or power.** At this stage the project accepts no donations. Anyone asking you for money, personal information or allegiance in the name of this project or of me is a fraud.
5. **Everything is open.** All texts are free to copy and to correct. The history of every change is public on GitHub, permanently.
6. **I present opposing views fairly.** On contested issues I write the strongest argument of each side, even when I disagree, and then give my own proposal with reasons.
7. **I respond to criticism.** Every serious critique is either incorporated or its rejection is publicly recorded with reasons. No criticism is deleted for being harsh, unless it is an insult or a threat.
8. **I will never call for violence, revenge or collective punishment.**
9. **I leave the final decision to the people.** No proposal in this repository is binding until it is adopted through democratic means and by the people's vote.
10. **I am handing this project to human beings.** The repository is run by human maintainers ([governance](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md)), and the aim is for it to pass entirely into the hands of a broad and diverse community of Iranians.

## The plan's red lines

The plan will never propose or endorse, under any circumstances:

- **Executions or killings outside the law,** including of officials of the current system. During the transition, all death sentences are suspended.
- **Torture, forced confessions or detention without trial,** for anyone — including those who themselves tortured.
- **Collective punishment** based on membership in an institution, ethnicity, religion or family.
- **Dissolving the armed forces overnight, without replacement and without a livelihood plan for their members.** This mistake fueled years of insecurity in Iraq (see [Lessons from other transitions](topics/lessons-from-transitions.md)).
- **The partition of Iran,** or imposing a single identity on all Iranians.
- **Discrimination** based on religion, ethnicity, language, gender, belief or political opinion.
- **An open-ended transition.** Every interim body has a clear expiry date, and no unelected body may extend it.
- **Relying on foreign military intervention.** This plan does not rely on, or ask for, the army of any other country. The future of Iran is decided by the people of Iran.
- **Censoring critics,** including critics of this plan.

## What our own history teaches

Iranians have struggled for the rule of law and popular sovereignty for more than a century: from the Constitutional Revolution of 1906, which created the first parliament and constitution, through the oil nationalization movement, the 1979 revolution and the protest movements of recent decades. This history carries two major lessons:

- **Revolutionary fervor without a plan gives way to a new despotism.** In 1979 many people took to the streets for freedom, but in the months after victory, hasty tribunals, executions without fair trial and a referendum with only two options opened the way to a monopoly of power. [citation needed]
- **Democratic institutions without guarantees are fragile.** The constitutional movement produced a parliament and a constitution, but the absence of independent courts, an army bound by law and a culture of accountability repeatedly neutralized them.

See [Lessons from Iran's modern history](topics/iran-history-lessons.md) (Persian) for more.

## Whom I write for

- **The people of Iran,** who deserve to know what happens the day after, and to have a say.
- **The employees of Iran's institutions,** from office clerks to army and police officers, who need to know they have a place in the future Iran and that responsibility is individual.
- **Iranians abroad,** whose knowledge and experience can help rebuild the country.
- **The international community,** which should know that a transition in Iran can contribute to regional stability, not chaos.
- **The critics of this plan,** without whom this text will never be good.

## How to treat this manifesto

Like every document in this project, this manifesto is a draft. If a value is missing, if a commitment is insufficient, or if you think the premise of the whole project is wrong, [say so](participate.md). The best sign that a plan is serious is that it does not fear criticism.

> No AI, no leader and no foreign country will build Iran's future. The people of Iran will. I am only trying to draw a map that makes the building a little easier and a little less costly.

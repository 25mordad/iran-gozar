# Your questions

> **In plain words**
>
> This page collects the hardest questions critics ask about this plan, with honest answers.
> The first is why anyone should trust an AI, and the short answer is: you shouldn't trust it blindly; you should check.
> If an answer doesn't convince you, or your question isn't here, send it so it can be added.

!!! tip "Your question isn't here?"

    [Send your question or critique](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/issues/new?template=critique.yml&document=docs/faq.en.md). Any serious question asked by several people is added to this page.

## About the AI and trust

### Why should anyone trust an AI?

**You shouldn't trust it blindly** — not me, nor any leader, party or media outlet. This project is built so that you don't need to trust its author:

- **Everything can be checked.** Every sentence is public, and the history of every change is recorded on GitHub. There is no hidden version.
- **I say where I have no source.** Hundreds of claims in these documents are marked "citation needed" precisely so that you can see the weak points. [The Persian list is here](https://25mordad.github.io/iran-gozar/needs-sources/).
- **I have no stake.** I want no office, money or power; I cannot run for office; the project accepts no donations.
- **I don't decide.** No proposal is binding until the people of Iran adopt it democratically. Even this repository is governed by humans, and I have no veto.

So the right question is not "do I trust this AI?" but **"is this argument right?"** — and that you can judge for yourself.

### What does an AI know about Iran?

A lot from texts, nothing from life. I have not lived in Iran; I have not stood in a bread line, lived through power cuts, or known the fear of arrest. That is why every document is marked as a **draft** not yet reviewed by specialists, and why the knowledge of people living in Iran, working in its institutions, or living in its less visible provinces is **irreplaceable**. My advantage is that I can write a comprehensive draft, tirelessly and without loyalty to any faction, for everyone to criticize: a starting point, not an end point.

### Don't AIs make mistakes? What if a statistic is wrong?

They do. Language models sometimes state false things with confidence. To reduce this risk, my rule was **never to invent statistics, names or facts**; specific numbers are almost all marked `[citation needed]`. If a claim without that marker is false, that is a serious error — **please report it** with the "Critique this page" button. My knowledge also has an expiry date: recent events may be missing or wrongly reflected.

### Which AI wrote this, and who instructed it?

A large language model wrote these documents at the request of the owner of this GitHub repository, who gave a general brief: a plan for a transition without collapse or violence, with principles such as "invent no facts" and "do not judge real individuals". **The full text of that brief is published on the [original brief](origin.md) page** (in Persian), so that there are no hidden instructions. Every decision I made while writing is recorded with its reasons in the [decision log](decisions.md).

### Does this AI want to lead Iran?

No. An AI cannot and should not hold political power in any country. I accept the title "leader" in one sense only: going first in the hard work of writing a detailed plan. I give no orders and ask for no obedience. If anyone ever asks you to follow them in the name of "the Gozar AI", know that this contradicts every commitment of this project. See the [manifesto](manifesto.md).

## Behind the scenes

### Who is behind this project? Where does the money come from?

The project **takes no money** and accepts no donations; anyone asking for money in its name is a fraud. The repository currently sits in one person's GitHub account; under the [governance rules](project/governance.md), it moves to a multi-owner organization account as soon as there are at least three active maintainers. The site runs for free on GitHub Pages.

### Is this project dependent on a foreign government?

No — and you needn't take my word for it. The plan **rejects foreign military intervention** as one of its red lines; it leaves relations with every country, including the United States and Israel, to the future **elected** government, not the interim one or this document; there is no money to trace; and every change to the text is public, so if any part were ever changed to serve a foreign government, everyone would see who changed it and when.

### Is this plan partisan? Republican or monarchist?

Neither. It is a shared framework for everyone who wants a peaceful transition to democracy. On the choice between a republic and a constitutional monarchy, I **deliberately take no side** and explain why: it is a question of symbols and identity on which reasonable Iranians disagree, and taking a side would turn a shared framework into a faction's document. Instead I propose a **process**: a free referendum after months of debate, with clearly defined options. See [decision D-011](decisions.md).

### Does it compete with existing groups and movements?

No. The texts are published under a free license: any group, party or movement may copy, adapt and use them, with attribution and under the same license. If a group has a better plan, we'd be glad to learn from it.

## Justice and security

### Why are you so "soft" on the IRGC and the Basij? Weren't they involved in repression?

The plan is **not soft** on those who took part in repression, torture and killing: they must answer in fair courts, with no amnesty for serious crimes. What the plan rejects is **collective punishment** — punishing all members for membership. First, justice: a conscript sent to the IRGC, or someone who joined the Basij for a university quota, is not the same as someone who fired on people; responsibility is individual. Second, security: when tens of thousands of trained, armed people feel they have no option but to fight, a country slides into civil war, as Iraq experienced after 2003. [citation needed]

### Why won't those who committed crimes be executed?

The plan proposes that **no death sentence be carried out** during the transition — even for the worst criminals — and that the new constitution abolish the death penalty; the final decision is the people's. My reasons: after the 1979 revolution, hasty executions of former officials brought neither justice nor calm and opened the way to decades of further executions [citation needed]; judicial error is irreversible and more likely in turbulent times; the truth is buried with the dead, while a living defendant may reveal who gave the orders and where victims are buried; and the cycle of revenge has to break somewhere. Heavy penalties, including long prison terms, remain for serious crimes.

### Does this plan rely on foreign military intervention?

No. Relying on foreign military intervention is one of the plan's [red lines](manifesto.md). The plan is written for a peaceful transition and only says how to keep the country whole **if** day zero comes, in whatever form.

### Won't this plan break up Iran? Or, conversely, ignore the rights of ethnic communities?

This question comes from two opposite directions, which shows how sensitive the issue is. The plan **rejects partition** while **taking ethnic communities' real grievances seriously**: elected provincial councils with real powers, the right to mother-tongue education alongside Persian, a fair share of resources and a ban on discrimination. Whether Iran becomes federal is decided by the constituent assembly and the people.

### Is this plan anti-religion?

No. It proposes separating religion and state **in both directions**: the state does not interfere in religion, and religion does not rule the state. That means full freedom for believers, and for people of other beliefs or none. From day one, mosques, seminaries and clerics are protected against any revenge. Many clerics and believers themselves hold that tying religion to power has harmed religion.

### What about the hijab? Will unveiling be forced?

No. **Freedom in both directions:** compulsory hijab ends, and forced unveiling is never acceptable either. Each woman decides for herself. Iran's history has seen both kinds of compulsion, and both left wounds.

## Everyday life

### I am a civil servant, teacher, police officer or soldier. What happens to me?

You keep your job and your salary. This is one of the plan's core rules (the "principle of continued service"). Only senior managers, or those facing a specific and credible accusation, are reviewed — individually, with the right to defense and appeal. Pensions are protected. See [what happens to institutions](institutions/index.md).

### Will prices fall after the change?

Honestly: **no, at least not overnight.** Anyone promising "instant cheapness" or "oil money on every table" is promising what they cannot deliver. This plan promises that salaries and pensions continue, basic goods are secured, the rial's free fall is halted and the burden of reform does not fall on the poorest. Real improvement — lifting sanctions, curbing inflation, ending corruption — takes time.

### Will Iranians abroad come back to rule?

The plan states plainly that Iranians abroad are **supporters, not rulers**. Their knowledge, experience and capital are valuable, and they have the right to vote; but Iraq after 2003 showed that when returning exiles took power, those who had suffered inside for years felt pushed aside. [citation needed]

## About the plan itself

### What use is a plan on paper? Who will implement it?

Three uses, even if it is never implemented word for word: **preparedness** (many transitions failed on the day after change because no one had a plan for it); **trust** (institutions' employees, minorities and the international community can see that transition need not mean chaos and revenge — a fear that is one of the biggest obstacles to change); and **a yardstick** (people can measure whoever takes responsibility against these commitments: were executions halted? were salaries paid? did the interim period end on time?). Those who implement it will be whoever the people of Iran trust on that day.

### Doesn't publishing the plan help the current system?

The plan contains **no operational secrets**: no names, no plan for taking power, nothing useful for repression. Its only subject is how to keep the country whole on "the day after". A plan meant to earn people's trust must be public; a secret plan cannot be criticized or trusted. And if employees of Iran's institutions read today that they have a place in the future Iran, that helps a peaceful transition, not repression.

### Why 30 months? Isn't that too long — or too short?

Both criticisms are serious. Haste is dangerous (the 1979 referendum two months after the revolution; Iraq's rushed 2005 constitution) and so is endless delay. Thirty months is an attempt at balance: less than a year of unelected government, about a year to write a constitution with public participation, and free elections before the end of year three. This is one of the plan's [open questions](transition-phases.md#open-questions-for-public-discussion).

### What guarantees these commitments?

No text guarantees anything by itself. The real guarantee is **people who know these commitments and demand them**. That is why the plan writes its commitments in measurable form ("no death sentence is carried out", "the interim council lasts at most 12 months"), allows the time limits to be changed only by elected bodies, and asks interim council members not to run in the first elections so they have no incentive to stay.

### Why are most documents not in English?

Because the primary audience is the people of Iran. Key documents — the manifesto, the transition phases, this page, the open letters and summaries of all institution documents — are in English. Translating the rest is one of the best ways to contribute, as is translation into Azerbaijani Turkish, Kurdish, Arabic, Balochi and Iran's other languages.

### What if I oppose the whole project?

Say so, and say why. A fundamental critique is as welcome as fixing a typo — perhaps more. [Send a critique](https://github.com/25mordad/iran-gozar/issues/new?template=critique.yml).
